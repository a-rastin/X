Indications for Lamictal, Lamictal ODT, Lamictal CD, and Subvenite

Indicated as adjunctive therapy for epilepsy in patients with
Partial-onset seizures

Primary generalized tonic-clonic (PGTC) seizures

Generalized seizures of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome

Indications for Lamictal XR

Indicated as adjunctive therapy for epilepsy in patients with
PGTC

Partial-onset seizures with or without secondary generalization

Dose escalation regimens (Lamictal, Lamictal ODT, Lamictal CD, Subvenite)

Patients taking valproate
Weeks 1-2: 25 mg PO every other day

Weeks 3-4: 25 mg PO daily

Week 5 onward to maintenance: Increase by 25-50 mg/day every 1-2 weeks

Usual maintenance dose: 100-200 mg/day with valproate alone; 100-400
mg/day with valproate and other drugs that induce glucuronidation; may give
once daily or divide in 2 doses

Patients NOT taking glucuronidation-inducing AEDs or valproate
Glucuronidation-inducing antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) include: carbamazepine,
phenytoin, phenobarbital, or primidone

Weeks 1-2: 25 mg PO daily

Usual maintenance dose: 225-375 mg PO daily in 2 divided doses

Also consider other glucuronidation-inducing drugs (eg, rifampin, ritonavir)

Weeks 1-2: 50 mg PO daily

Weeks 3-4: 100 mg/day PO in divided doses

Week 5 onward to maintenance: Increase by 100 mg/day every 1-2 weeks

Conversion from adjunctive therapy to monotherapy

Goal of transition regimen is to attempt to maintain seizure control while mitigating
risk of serious rash associated with rapid lamotrigine titration

To avoid an increased risk of rash, do not exceed recommended initial lamotrigine
dose and subsequent dose escalations

Recommended monotherapy maintenance dosage range
Lamictal, Lamictal ODT, Lamictal CD, Subvenite: 500 mg/day PO in 2 divided
doses

Lamictal XR: 250-300 mg PO daily

Conversion from adjunctive therapy with carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital,

or primidone to lamotrigine monotherapy

Lamictal, Lamictal ODT, Lamictal CD, Subvenite
After gradually achieving the recommended monotherapy maintenance dose
of 250 mg BID, withdraw enzyme-inducing AEDs by 20% decrements each
week over a 4-weeks

Lamictal XR
After gradually achieving a dose of 500 mg/day, withdraw enzyme-inducing
AEDs by 20% decrements each week over a 4-weeks

2 weeks after completing withdrawal of enzyme-inducing AEDs, decrease
Lamictal XR no faster than 100 mg/day each week to achieve maintenance
dosage range of 250-300 mg/day

Conversion from adjunctive therapy with valproate to lamotrigine monotherapy

Lamictal, Lamictal ODT, Lamictal CD, Subvenite
Step 1: Achieve lamotrigine dose of 200 mg/day; maintain established stable
dose of valproate

Step 2: Maintain lamotrigine at 200 mg/day; decrease valproate by decrements
≤500 mg/day/week to 500 mg/day and then maintain for 1 week

Step 3: Increase lamotrigine to 150 mg BID; simultaneously decrease
valproate to 250 mg/day and maintain for 1 week

Step 4: Increase lamotrigine by 100 mg/day weekly to achieve maintenance
dose of 250 mg BID; discontinue valproate

Lamictal XR
Step 1: Achieve Lamictal XR dose of 150 mg/day; maintain established stable
dose of valproate

Step 2: Maintain Lamictal XR at 150 mg/day; decrease valproate by
decrements <=500 mg/day/week to 500 mg/day and then maintain for 1 week

Step 3: Increase Lamictal XR to 200 mg/day; simultaneously decrease
valproate to 250 mg/day and maintain for 1 week

Bipolar Disorder

Indicated for maintenance treatment of bipolar I disorder to delay time to occurrence of
mood episodes (depression, mania, hypomania, mixed episodes) in adults treated for
acute mood episodes with standard therapy

Limitations of use: Efficacy and safety have not been established for treatment of acute
manic or mixed episodes

Dose is gradually escalated to target dose to avoid an increased risk of rash

Dosage Modifications

Renal impairment

Base initial dose on patients' concomitant medications

Reduced maintenance doses may be effective for patients with significant renal
impairment (plasma half-life of lamotrigine was ~twice as long in in patients with
chronic renal failure)

Few patients with severe renal impairment have been evaluated during chronic
treatment

Caution in patients with significant renal impairment owing to inadequate
experience in this population

Hepatic impairment

Mild: No dosage adjustment needed

Moderate-to-severe (without ascites): Reduce initial, escalation, and maintenance
doses by ~25%

Severe with ascites: Reduce initial, escalation, and maintenance doses by ~50%;
adjust escalation and maintenance doses according to clinical response

Pregnancy & Lactation

Pregnancy

A pregnancy exposure registry monitors pregnancy outcomes in women exposed to
AEDs, including lamotrigine, during pregnancy; encourage women who are taking
lamotrigine during pregnancy to enroll in the North American Antiepileptic Drug
(NAAED) Pregnancy Registry by calling 1-888-233-2334 or visiting
https://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org/

Data from several prospective pregnancy exposure registries and epidemiological
studies of pregnant women have not detected increased frequency of major congenital
malformations or consistent pattern of malformations among women exposed to
lamotrigine compared with the general population; the majority of exposure data are
from women with epilepsy

As with other AEDs, physiological changes during pregnancy may affect lamotrigine
concentrations and/or therapeutic effect; there have been reports of decreased
lamotrigine concentrations during pregnancy and restoration of pre-pregnancy
concentrations after delivery; dose adjustments may be necessary to maintain clinical
response

Lactation

Drug is present in milk from lactating women receiving therapy; neonates and young
infants are at risk for high serum levels because maternal serum and milk levels can rise
to high levels postpartum if lamotrigine dosage has been increased during pregnancy
but is not reduced to pre-pregnancy dosage after delivery

Glucuronidation is required for drug clearance; glucuronidation capacity is immature in
the infant and this may also contribute to level of lamotrigine exposure; events including
rash, apnea, drowsiness, poor sucking, and poor weight gain (requiring hospitalization in
some cases) reported in infants who have been human milk-fed by mothers using
lamotrigine; whether or not these events were caused by lamotrigine is unknown; no
data are available on effects of drug on milk production

Developmental and health benefits of breastfeeding should be considered along with
mother’s clinical need for therapy and any potential adverse effects on breastfed infant
from drug or from the underlying maternal condition

Human milk-fed infants should be closely monitored for adverse events resulting from
lamotrigine; measurement of infant serum levels should be performed to rule out toxicity
if concerns arise; human milk-feeding should be discontinued in infants with lamotrigine
toxicity
