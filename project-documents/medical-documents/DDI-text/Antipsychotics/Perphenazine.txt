## Dosing & Uses [cite: 7]

### ADULT [cite: 8]

Intractable Hiccoughs (Off-label) [cite: 15]

* Maximum: 24 mg [cite: 17]

Treatment of Nausea/Vomiting [cite: 18]
* 8-16 mg PO qDay divided q6-12hr [cite: 19]
* Maximum: 24 mg [cite: 20]

Schizophrenia [cite: 25]
* Hospitalized patients: 8-16mg PO q6-12hr [cite: 26]
* Hospitalized patients: Not to exceed 64 mg/day divided q6-12hr [cite: 27]
* Outpatients: 4-8mg PO q8hr; reduce as soon as possible to minimum effective dose [cite: 28]

Hepatic Impairment [cite: 29]
* Dose adjustment not described in manufacturer's labeling [cite: 30]

Renal Impairment [cite: 31]
* Dose adjustment not described in manufacturer's labeling [cite: 32]

### PEDIATRIC [cite: 33]
Dosage Forms & Strengths: tablets (2mg, 4mg, 8mg, 16mg) [cite: 34, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39]

Intractable Hiccoughs (Off-label) [cite: 40]
* <12 years: Not recommended by manufacturer [cite: 41, 42]
* >12 years: 8-16 mg PO qDay divided q8-12hr; Maximum: 24 mg [cite: 43, 44]

Schizophrenia [cite: 45]
* <12 years: Not recommended by manufacturer [cite: 46, 47]
* >12 years:
  - Hospitalized patients: 8-16mg PO q6-12hr [cite: 49]
  - Hospitalized patients: Not to exceed 64 mg/day divided q6-12hr [cite: 50]
  - Outpatients: 4-8mg PO q8hr; reduce as soon as possible to minimum effective dose [cite: 51]

### GERIATRIC [cite: 56]
* Initiate dosing at lower end of the range [cite: 57]
* Intractable Hiccoughs: 8-16 mg PO qDay divided q8-12hr; Maximum: 24 mg [cite: 58, 59, 60]
* Treatment of Nausea/Vomiting: 8-16 mg PO qDay divided q6-12hr; Maximum: 24 mg [cite: 61, 62, 63]
* Schizophrenia: Hospitalized patients 8-16mg PO q6-12hr (Max 64 mg/day divided q6-12hr); Outpatients 4-8mg PO q8hr, reduce to minimum effective dose [cite: 64, 65, 66, 67]

## Warnings [cite: 1964]

### Black Box Warnings [cite: 1965]
* Patients with dementia-related psychosis who are treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death as shown in short-term controlled trials. [cite: 1966]
* The deaths appeared to be either cardiovascular (eg, heart failure, sudden death) or infectious (eg, pneumonia) in nature [cite: 1967]
* This drug is not approved for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis [cite: 1968]

### Contraindications [cite: 1969]
* Documented hypersensitivity to phenothiazines [cite: 1970]
* Coma, severe hypotension, severe CNS depression, concurrency with large amounts of CNS depressants, poorly controlled seizure disorder, subcortical brain damage with or without hypothalamic damage, myelosuppression, liver damage, blood dyscrasias [cite: 1971, 1976]
* Severe cardiovascular disease [cite: 1977]
* Lactation [cite: 1978]

### Cautions [cite: 1979]
* Avoid using in children with suspected Reye's syndrome [cite: 1980]
* Use caution in prostatic hypertrophy, stenosing PUD, tardive dyskinesia, hypocalcemia, renal/hepatic impairment, patients who have exhibited a severe reaction to insulin or ECT, history of seizures, asthma, respiratory tract infections, cardiovascular disease [cite: 1981]
* Perphenazine products can lower convulsive threshold; use caution in alcohol withdrawal or convulsive disorders (may require increased anticonvulsant dosage) [cite: 1982, 1983]
* Use with caution in psychiatric depression; monitor for suicide risk until remission occurs; do not give access to large quantities [cite: 1984, 1985, 1986]
* Caution if severe adverse reactions to other phenothiazines occurred; keep under close supervision [cite: 1987, 1988]
* Discontinue if significant, unexplained rise in body temperature occurs [cite: 1989]
* Monitor for hypotension in surgical patients; reduced anesthetics/CNS depressants may be needed [cite: 1990, 1991]
* Discontinue if hepatic test abnormalities or abnormal BUN occur [cite: 1992, 1993]
* Use with caution in respiratory impairment (acute infections, severe asthma, emphysema) [cite: 1994]
* Avoid abrupt cessation to prevent gastritis, nausea, vomiting, dizziness; taper with antiparkinson agents [cite: 1995, 1996]
* Long-term therapy risks: liver damage, corneal/lenticular deposits, irreversible dyskinesias [cite: 1997]
* Avoid undue sun exposure due to photosensitivity [cite: 1998]

### Specific Condition Warnings
Leukopenia and neutropenia [cite: 1999]
* Events of leukopenia/neutropenia and agranulocytosis reported [cite: 2000]
* Risk factors include preexisting low WBC and history of drug induced leukopenia/neutropenia [cite: 2001, 2005]
* Monitor CBC frequently during first few months; discontinue at first sign of WBC decline [cite: 2006, 2007]
* Periodic blood checks required; stop drug if blood dyscrasias appear [cite: 2008]

Tardive dyskinesia [cite: 2009]
* Risk increases with duration of treatment and cumulative dose, though can develop after brief low-dose treatment [cite: 2010, 2011]
* Prescribe to minimize occurrence; reserve chronic treatment for responsive illnesses lacking safer alternatives [cite: 2012, 2013]
* Use smallest dose and shortest duration; reassess periodically [cite: 2014, 2015]
* Consider discontinuation if signs appear, though some may require continued treatment [cite: 2016, 2017]

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome [cite: 2018]
* Manifestations: hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability [cite: 2019]
* Management: immediate discontinuation, symptomatic treatment, medical monitoring [cite: 2020]
* No specific pharmacologic treatment universally agreed upon [cite: 2021]
* Reintroduction must be carefully considered and monitored; recurrences reported [cite: 2022, 2023]
* Do not use epinephrine for hypotension (action is blocked/reversed); use norepinephrine or dopamine [cite: 2024, 2025]
* Severe hypotension likely in mitral insufficiency or pheochromocytoma; rebound hypertension possible in latter [cite: 2026, 2030, 2032]

Drug interaction overview [cite: 2033]
* Potentiates CNS depressants; lower dosages of added drugs recommended [cite: 2034]
* Caution with atropine or extreme heat/phosphorus insecticides (additive anticholinergic effects) [cite: 2035]
* Avoid alcohol due to additive effects, hypotension, and increased suicide/overdose risk [cite: 2036, 2037, 2038]

## Pharmacology [cite: 2049]

Mechanism of Action [cite: 2050]
* Antipsychotic that blocks postsynaptic mesolimbic dopaminergic receptors in the brain; it has moderate anticholinergic effects, weak to moderate sedative effects, strong extrapyramidal effects, and strong antiemetic activity [cite: 2051, 2052, 2053]

Pharmacokinetics [cite: 2057]
* Peak Plasma Time: 1-3 hr; 2-4 hr (metabolite) [cite: 2059]
* Half-Life: 9-12 hr; 10-19 hr (metabolite) [cite: 2060]
* Concentration: 984 pg/mL; 509 pg/mL (metabolite) [cite: 2061]
* Metabolism: Hepatic P450 enzyme CYP2D6 [cite: 2062]
* Metabolites: 7-hydroxyperphenazine [cite: 2063]
* Enzymes inhibited: CYP2D6 [cite: 2064]
* Excretion: Urine and feces [cite: 2065]
