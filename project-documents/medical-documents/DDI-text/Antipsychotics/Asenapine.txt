# Asenapine (Saphris)

## Drug Classification
* **Brand Name:** Saphris[cite: 5].
* **Class:** Antipsychotics, 2nd Generation[cite: 5].

---

## Dosing & Uses

### Adult
* **Dosage Forms:** Sublingual tablet (2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg)[cite: 9, 10, 11, 12].
* **Schizophrenia:** 5 mg SL q12hr initially; maintenance: after 1 week, may be increased up to 10 mg PO q12hr[cite: 13, 14].
* **Bipolar Disorder (Manic or mixed episodes):** * *Monotherapy:* 10 mg PO q12hr initially; may be decreased to 5 mg PO q12hr on day 2 and subsequent days if warranted by adverse effects or individual tolerance (90% typically remain on higher dose)[cite: 16, 17].
  * *Adjunct to lithium or valproate:* 5 mg PO q12hr initially; may be increased to 10 mg PO q12hr if warranted[cite: 18].
  * *Duration:* If response is favorable, continue beyond initial acute phase[cite: 19].

### Pediatric
* **Dosage Forms:** Sublingual tablet (2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg)[cite: 32, 33, 34, 35].
* **Bipolar Disorder (Manic or mixed episodes):**
  * *<10 years:* Safety and efficacy not established[cite: 38].
  * *10-17 years:* 2.5 mg SL q12hr initially; may increase to 5 mg SL q12hr after 3 days, and to 10 mg SL q12hr after 3 additional days[cite: 39].
* **Dosing Considerations:**
  * Pediatric patients are more sensitive to dystonia with initial dosing when the recommended escalation schedule is not followed[cite: 41].
  * Safety and efficacy of doses >10 mg q12hr are not established[cite: 42].

### Geriatric
* Not indicated for dementia-related psychosis due to increased risk of death compared to placebo[cite: 44].
* In elderly patients with psychosis, asenapine exposure (AUC) was on average 40% higher than in younger adults[cite: 45].

### Dosage Modifications
* **Renal impairment:** Dose adjustment not necessary[cite: 25, 26].
* **Hepatic impairment:** * *Mild to moderate (Child-Pugh A or B):* Dose adjustment not necessary[cite: 27, 28].
  * *Severe (Child-Pugh C):* Contraindicated[cite: 29].

## Warnings & Cautions

### Black Box Warnings
* Not indicated for dementia-related psychosis[cite: 1687]. 
* Increased risk of death in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis[cite: 1688]. 
* Placebo-controlled trials with other atypical antipsychotics showed a higher incidence of cerebrovascular adverse reactions (e.g., CVAs, TIAs), including fatalities, compared with placebo[cite: 1689].

### Contraindications
* Known hypersensitivity[cite: 1691].
* Severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C)[cite: 1692].

### Cautions
* Type 1 hypersensitivity reactions (anaphylaxis and angioedema)[cite: 1694, 1695].
* Neuroleptic malignant syndrome[cite: 1696].
* Hyperglycemia; monitor fasting plasma glucose[cite: 1697, 1698].
* Weight gain and metabolic changes; monitor weight frequently[cite: 1699, 1700, 1719, 1720, 1721].
* Hypotension and syncope[cite: 1701, 1702, 1722].
* Leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis; perform CBC[cite: 1703, 1704].
* Concurrent use of CNS-acting drugs or alcohol[cite: 1705].
* Caution in patients with a history of seizures or lower seizure thresholds[cite: 1706, 1707].
* Cognitive or motor impairment (somnolence, falls)[cite: 1708, 1709, 1723, 1724].
* Dysphagia, dysmotility, and aspiration[cite: 1710].
* Disruption of body temperature regulation[cite: 1714, 1715, 1716].
* Not recommended with severe hepatic impairment[cite: 1717].
* Inherent suicide risk warrants close supervision[cite: 1718].
* Extrapyramidal symptoms and Tardive dyskinesia[cite: 1725, 1744, 1745, 1746, 1747, 1748, 1749, 1750, 1751].
* Hyperprolactinemia (elevates prolactin levels, potentially inhibiting reproductive function or causing galactorrhea, amenorrhea, gynecomastia, and impotence)[cite: 1726, 1727, 1728, 1729, 1730, 1731, 1732].
* QT prolongation (avoid with Class 1A/3 antiarrhythmics, other QT-prolonging drugs, or in patients with arrhythmias, hypokalemia, or hypomagnesemia)[cite: 1739, 1741, 1742, 1743].

## Pregnancy & Lactation

### Pregnancy
* Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms[cite: 1756].
* No teratogenicity observed in animal reproduction studies at 0.7 and 0.4 times the MRHD (rats and rabbits, respectively)[cite: 1761, 1765].
* Increased post-implantation loss, early pup deaths, and decreased pup survival/weight gain noted in rats[cite: 1766, 1767]. 
* Monitor neonates for withdrawal symptoms (agitation, hypertonia, tremor, somnolence, respiratory distress)[cite: 1770, 1771, 1772, 1773].

### Lactation
* Studies have not assessed presence in human milk; however, drug is excreted in rat milk[cite: 1775, 1776]. 

### Pregnancy Categories (Definitions)
* **A:** Generally acceptable[cite: 1778].
* **B:** May be acceptable[cite: 1779].
* **C:** Use with caution if benefits outweigh risks[cite: 1780].
* **D:** Use in LIFE-THREATENING emergencies[cite: 1781].
* **X:** Do not use in pregnancy[cite: 1782].
* **NA:** Information not available[cite: 1783].

## Pharmacology

* **Mechanism of Action:** Unknown; efficacy is thought to be mediated via combined antagonist activity at dopamine D2 and serotonin type 2 (5-HT2) receptors[cite: 1789, 1790].
* **Absorption:** * Bioavailability: Sublingual, 35%; swallowed, ≤2%[cite: 1792]. 
  * Peak plasma time: 0.5-1.5 hr[cite: 1793]. 
  * Peak plasma concentration: 4 ng/mL[cite: 1794].
* **Distribution:** Protein bound: 95%[cite: 1796]. Vd: 20-25 L/kg[cite: 1797].
* **Metabolism:** Metabolized by UGT1A4 and CYP450 (predominantly isoenzyme 1A2)[cite: 1799]. Inhibits CYP2D6 (weakly)[cite: 1800].
* **Elimination:** Half-life: 24 hr[cite: 1802]. Clearance: 52 L/hr[cite: 1803]. Excretion: Urine (50%), feces (40%)[cite: 1804].

## Administration

* **Instructions:** Sublingual tablet; to allow optimal absorption, place under tongue and allow to dissolve completely (dissolves within seconds)[cite: 1807].
* Do not chew, split, crush, or swallow sublingual tablet[cite: 1808].
* Do not eat or drink for at least 10 minutes after administration[cite: 1809].
