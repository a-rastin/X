thioridazine (Rx)
Brand and Other Names: Mellaril
Classes: Antipsychotics, Phenothiazines

Dosing & Uses

ADULT
Dosage Forms & Strengths
tablet: • 10mg • 25mg • 50mg • 100mg

Schizophrenia
Initial 50-100 mg PO q8hr, THEN 200-800 mg/day PO divided q6-12hr

Depressive Disorders
25 mg PO q8hr; may titrate to effect (20-200 mg/day)

Renal Impairment
Dose adjustment not necessary in dialysis

Hepatic Disease
Initiate treatment at lower dose and titrate to effect (monitor)

Other Indications & Uses
Psychotic disorders, geriatric psychoneurotic manifestations, pediatric behavioral disorders

PEDIATRIC
Dosage Forms & Strengths
tablet: • 10mg • 25mg • 50mg • 100mg

Schizophrenia
<2 years: Safety and efficacy not established
2-12 years: 0.5-3 mg/kg/day divided q8hr PO, no more than 3 mg/kg/day
>12 years: Initial 50-100 mg PO q8hr; titrate to 200-800 mg/day PO divided q6-12hr
Potential toxic dose <6 years old: 3 mg/kg

GERIATRIC
Initiate treatment at lower dose and titrate to effect (monitor)

Schizophrenia
Initial 50-100 mg PO q8hr, THEN 200-800 mg/day PO divided q6-12hr
Debilitated patients: Initiate treatment at lower dose

Depressive disorders
25 mg PO q8hr; may titrate to effect (20-200 mg/day)

Serious (146)
• Multiple drugs interact with thioridazine by increasing the QTc interval (e.g., amiodarone, amitriptyline, amoxapine, arsenic trioxide, chlorpromazine, citalopram, clarithromycin, dofetilide, droperidol, encorafenib, entrectinib, erythromycin, fluconazole, haloperidol, methadone, moxifloxacin, ondansetron, ziprasidone, etc.). Avoid or Use Alternate Drug.
• Multiple drugs interact via CYP2D6 metabolism affecting levels of thioridazine (e.g., adagrasib, amiodarone, artemether/lumefantrine, asenapine, bupropion, celecoxib, chloroquine, cimetidine, dacomitinib, duloxetine, fluoxetine, haloperidol, imatinib, paroxetine, propafenone, ritonavir). Avoid or Use Alternate Drug.
• CNS depressants / Opioids (e.g., benzhydrocodone/acetaminophen, buprenorphine, fentanyl, hydrocodone, remifentanil): Either increases toxicity of the other by pharmacodynamic synergism (profound sedation, respiratory depression, coma, death). Avoid or Use Alternate Drug.
• Dopamine antagonists/agonists (e.g., apomorphine, bromocriptine, cabergoline, dopamine, levodopa, pramipexole, ropinirole, methyldopa): Thioridazine decreases effects by pharmacodynamic antagonism. Contraindicated / Avoid or Use Alternate Drug.

Monitor Closely (356)
• Abiraterone, cobicistat, darifenacin, elvitegravir, mirabegron, panobinostat, terbinafine: Alters metabolism via CYP2D6. Modify Therapy/Monitor Closely.
• Anticholinergics/Sedatives (e.g., diphenhydramine, alprazolam, diazepam, zolpidem, gabapentin): Additive sedation and anticholinergic effects. Use Caution/Monitor.
• Other QTc prolonging agents requiring close monitoring and ECG evaluation (e.g., bedaquiline, ciprofloxacin, dasatinib, foscarnet, lapatinib, levofloxacin, paliperidone, ranolazine, romidepsin). Use Caution/Monitor.
• Stimulants/Sympathomimetics (e.g., amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, methylphenidate, epinephrine, pseudoephedrine): Effect on sedation is unclear. Risk of cardiac arrhythmia or sudden death. Use Caution/Monitor.

Minor (64)
• Minor interactions typically involve additive anticholinergic effects, mild changes in metabolism (e.g., chloroquine, ethanol, codeine, tramadol), or minor pharmacodynamic antagonism/synergism (e.g., amantadine, captopril). Minor/Significance Unknown.

Warnings
Black Box Warnings
• Patients with dementia-related psychosis who are treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death as shown in short-term controlled trials; the deaths appeared to be either cardiovascular (eg, heart failure, sudden death) or infectious (eg, pneumonia) in nature; this drug is not approved for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis.
• Shown to prolong QTc interval in a dose-related manner; drugs with this potential have been associated with Torsades de pointes type arrhythmias and sudden death; due to potential for significant, possibly life-threatening, proarrhythmic effects, therapy should be reserved for use in treatment of schizophrenic patients who fail to show acceptable response to adequate courses of treatment with other antipsychotic drugs.

Contraindications
• Documented hypersensitivity to phenothiazines
• Coadministration with cytochrome P450 2D6 isozyme inhibitors (eg, fluoxetine and paroxetine) and certain other drugs (eg, fluvoxamine, propranolol, and pindolol)
• Patients who are known to have a genetic defect leading to reduced levels of activity of P450 2D6
• Patients with hypertensive or hypotensive heart disease of extreme degree
• Patients in comatose state
• Severe CNS depression
• Severe hyper-/hypotensive heart disease

Cautions
• Avoid using in children with suspected Reye's syndrome
• Hypotension may be particularly severe in patients with pheochromocytoma or mitral insufficiency
• Depresses hypothalamic thermoregulatory mechanism; exposure to extreme temperatures may cause hypo- or hyperthermia
• Leukopenia and/or agranulocytosis and convulsive seizures reported but infrequent; in schizophrenic patients with epilepsy, anticonvulsant medication should be maintained during treatment
• Pigmentary retinopathy has been observed primarily in patients taking larger than recommended doses.
• Patients participating in activities requiring complete mental alertness (eg, driving) should be advised to administer cautiously.
• Female patients appear to have a greater tendency to orthostatic hypotension than male patients. Avoid epinephrine in treatment of drug-induced hypotension.
• Prolactin levels may be elevated.
• Proarrhythmic effects: baseline ECG and serum potassium levels should be measured. Discontinue if QTc interval is over 500 msec.
• Tardive dyskinesia: potentially irreversible, involuntary, dyskinetic movements may develop.
• Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS): potentially fatal symptom complex reported. Discontinue antipsychotic immediately if NMS occurs.

Pregnancy & Lactation
Pregnancy
• Category C (based on general descriptive context; specific letter category context listed below).
• Use during pregnancy only if potential benefit justifies potential risk to fetus.
• Neonates exposed to antipsychotic drugs during third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms following delivery.

Lactation
• Not known whether the drug is present in the milk; infants exposed should be monitored for signs of adverse effects; routine monitoring of infant serum concentrations not recommended.

Pharmacology
Mechanism of Action
Piperazine phenothiazine agent; antagonist for the postsynaptic mesolimbic dopaminergic D2 receptors in the brain; decreases the release of hypothalamic and hypophyseal hormones.

Pharmacokinetics
Half-Life elimination: 24 hr
Metabolism: Hepatic P450 enzyme CYP2D6
Enzymes inhibited: CYP2D6
Protein bound: 95%
Duration: 4-5 days
