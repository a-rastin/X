Monitor Closely (388)
*Note: Numerous medications require close monitoring when coadministered with Fluphenazine due to shared risks of QTc prolongation, enhanced CNS sedation, additive anticholinergic effects, or alterations via CYP2D6 metabolism. Key classes and notable examples include:*

• CNS Depressants & Sedatives: (e.g., alprazolam, buprenorphine, chlordiazepoxide, clonazepam, diazepam, eszopiclone, fentanyl, lorazepam, methadone, midazolam, morphine, oxycodone, propofol, zolpidem, ethanol/alcohol). Additive sedation, respiratory depression, coma.
• CYP2D6 Inhibitors & Substrates: (e.g., abiraterone, bupropion, clobazam, desvenlafaxine, eliglustat, fedratinib, lorcaserin, lumefantrine, terbinafine, venlafaxine). These can increase fluphenazine levels.
• QTc Prolonging Agents: (e.g., alfuzosin, amisulpride, asenapine, azithromycin, citalopram, clozapine, dasatinib, escitalopram, flecainide, gemifloxacin, methadone, olanzapine, paliperidone, quetiapine, risperidone). ECG monitoring closely recommended.
• Anticholinergic Agents: (e.g., atropine, benztropine, dicyclomine, diphenhydramine, glycopyrrolate, hyoscyamine, oxybutynin, scopolamine, tolterodine). Additive anticholinergic adverse effects. Decreased GI absorption of fluphenazine may occur.
• Dopamine Antagonists & Antipsychotics: (e.g., chlorpromazine, haloperidol, loxapine, metoclopramide, perphenazine, thiothixene). Increased risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) and neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS).
• Serotonin Modulators: (e.g., almotriptan, dextromethorphan, eletriptan, frovatriptan, sumatriptan). May enhance dopamine blockade, increasing NMS risk, or enhance serotonergic effects leading to serotonin syndrome.
• Sympathomimetics & Stimulants: (e.g., amphetamine, dexmethylphenidate, dopamine, epinephrine, norepinephrine). Fluphenazine may block the pressor response (resulting in severe hypotension) or interact with unknown mechanisms increasing risk of cardiac arrhythmias.

Warnings

Black Box Warnings
Patients with dementia-related psychosis who are treated with antipsychotic drugs are at increased risk for death, as shown in short-term controlled trials; deaths in these trials appeared to be either cardiovascular (eg, heart failure, sudden death) or infectious (eg, pneumonia) in nature. This drug is not approved for treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis.

Contraindications
• Documented hypersensitivity
• Coma, severe hypotension, severe central nervous system (CNS) depression, concurrent use of large amounts of CNS depressants, subcortical brain damage, poorly controlled seizure disorder
• Blood dyscrasias, severe cardiovascular disease, patients receiving large doses of hypnotics, children <12 years (decanoate dosage form only)

Cautions
• Use caution in glaucoma, prostatic hypertrophy, stenosing peptic ulcer disease (PUD), history of NMS, Parkinson disease, hypocalcemia, renal or hepatic impairment, history of severe reaction to insulin or electroconvulsive therapy (ECT), history of seizures, asthma, respiratory tract infections, cardiovascular disease
• May affect cardiac conduction, which may result in life-threatening arrhythmias
• Mild leukocytosis, leukopenia, and eosinophilia occasionally occur
• Dermatologic reactions are common
• May cause pigmentary retinopathy, and lenticular and corneal deposits, especially in prolonged therapy
• May impair core body temperature regulation; caution with strenuous exercise, dehydration, heat exposure, and concomitant medication possessing anticholinergic effects
• CNS depression may impair physical or mental abilities; use caution operating heavy machinery
• Watch for urinary retention, blurred vision, dry mouth, and constipation as result of anticholinergic effects
• Risk of extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), NMS, hypotension; hypotension may be particularly severe in patients with pheochromocytoma or mitral insufficiency
• May need anticholinergic antiparkinsonian agent to counter EPS; in case of severe hypotension, use norepinephrine or phenylepinephrine, and do not use epinephrine or dopamine
• Use caution in patients with urinary retention, paralytic ileus, decreased gastrointestinal motility, BPH, visual problems or xerostomia
• Depresses hypothalamic thermoregulatory mechanism; exposure to extreme temperatures may cause hypo- or hyperthermia
• May increase risk of falls, resulting from orthostatic hypotension, somnolence, and motor sensory instability; complete fall risk assessment patients on medications or with diseases that may increase fall risk
• Use associated with increased prolactin levels; clinical significance in patients with breast cancer or other prolactin-dependent tumors unknown
• US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) warning regarding off-label use for dementia in elderly

Pharmacology

Mechanism of Action
Phenothiazine; antagonizes dopaminergic D1 and D2 receptors; depresses release of hypothalamic and hypophyseal hormones

Absorption
• Bioavailability: Rapidly absorbed
• Onset: Hydrochloride salt, 1 hr; decanoate, 24-72 hr
• Duration: Hydrochloride salt, 6-8 hr; decanoate, 4 weeks
• Peak plasma time: Hydrochloride salt, 2 hr; decanoate, 8-10 hr
• Peak effect: Decanoate, 48-96 hr

Elimination
• Half-life: Hydrochloride salt, 14-16 hr; decanoate, 14 days
• Excretion: Urine, feces
