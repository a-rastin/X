olanzapine (Rx) [cite: 3]
Brand and Other Names: Zyprexa, Zyprexa Relprevv, Zyprexa Zydis [cite: 4]
Classes: Antipsychotics, 2nd Generation; Antimanic Agents [cite: 4]

DOSING & USES [cite: 5]
ADULT [cite: 6]
Dosage Forms & Strengths [cite: 7]
• tablet (Zyprexa, generic): 2.5mg, 5mg, 7.5mg, 10mg, 15mg, 20mg [cite: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]
• tablet, orally disintegrating (Zyprexa Zydis): 5mg, 10mg, 15mg, 20mg [cite: 15, 16, 17, 18, 19]
• IM injection, short-acting (Zyprexa, generic): 10mg/vial [cite: 20, 21]
• IM injection, extended-release suspension (Zyprexa Relprevv): 210mg/vial, 300mg/vial, 405mg/vial [cite: 22, 23, 24, 25]

Schizophrenia [cite: 29]
Indicated for treatment of schizophrenia [cite: 31]
PO [cite: 32]
Dose selection: 5-10 mg daily, with a target dose of 10 mg/day within several days [cite: 33, 34]
If further dosage adjustments are indicated, wait at least 1 week; steady state not typically achieved until ~1 week [cite: 35]
• Adjust dose increments/decrements by 5 mg daily [cite: 36]
• Efficacy in schizophrenia was demonstrated in dose range of 10-15 mg/day in clinical trials; however, doses >10 mg/day did not demonstrate greater efficacy than 10 mg/day [cite: 37, 38]
• Doses above target dose (ie, >=15 mg/day) are recommended only after clinical assessment [cite: 39]
• Not indicated at doses >20 mg/day [cite: 40]
• Special populations: Patients who are debilitated, who have a predisposition to hypotensive reactions, who otherwise exhibit a combination of factors that may result in slower metabolism of olanzapine (eg, nonsmoking female patients >=65 years), or who may be more pharmacodynamically sensitive to olanzapine [cite: 41, 42]
  - Recommended starting dose: 5 mg daily [cite: 43]
  - Use caution with dose escalation [cite: 44]
• Maintenance treatment: 10-20 mg/day demonstrated efficacy in a placebo-controlled trial for maintenance of treatment response in adults with schizophrenia stabilized on olanzapine for at least 8 weeks prior to monitoring for relapse [cite: 45, 46]
• Periodically reevaluate long-term efficacy and need for maintenance treatment [cite: 47]

IM, extended-release (Zyprexa Relprevv) [cite: 48]
• Establish tolerability with oral olanzapine prior to use; IM extended-release dose determined by oral dose [cite: 49]
• Oral dosage 10 mg/day [cite: 50]
  - Initial 8 weeks: 210 mg IM every 2 weeks or 405 mg IM every 4 weeks [cite: 51]
  - Thereafter: 150 mg every 2 weeks or 300 mg every 4 weeks [cite: 52]
• Oral dosage 15 mg/day [cite: 53]
  - Initial 8 weeks: 300 mg IM every 2 weeks [cite: 54]
  - Thereafter: 210 mg every 2 weeks or 405 mg every 4 weeks [cite: 55]
• Oral dosage 20 mg/day [cite: 56]
  - Initial 8 weeks: 300 mg IM every 2 weeks [cite: 57]
  - Thereafter: 300 mg every 2 weeks [cite: 62]

Bipolar I Disorder (Manic or Mixed Episodes) [cite: 63]
Indicated for acute/maintenance treatment of manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar 1 disorder; may be used adjunctively to valproate or lithium in the treatment of manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar disorder [cite: 64, 65]
• Monotherapy: 10-15 mg/day PO initially; may adjust dose by increments/decrements of 5 mg/day at intervals >24 hr [cite: 66]
• Adjunct to lithium or valproate: 10 mg/day PO initially [cite: 67]
• Maintenance monotherapy: Maintain dose of 5-20 mg/day PO after achieving response for ~2 weeks; not to exceed 20 mg/day [cite: 68]

Agitation Associated with Schizophrenia and Bipolar I Mania [cite: 70]
Indicated for treatment of acute agitation associated with schizophrenia and bipolar I mania [cite: 71]
• IM, short-acting (Zyprexa): 2.5-10 mg/dose; additional doses (up to 10 mg) may be considered; administer subsequent doses 2 hr after initial dose and 4 hr after 2nd dose, if necessary; not to exceed 30 mg/day [cite: 72, 73]

Bipolar I Disorder (Depressive Episodes) [cite: 74]
Indicated for depressive episodes associated with bipolar I disorder in combination with fluoxetine [cite: 75]
• 5 mg PO in evening; adjusted to range of 5-12.5 mg/day [cite: 76]
Safety of coadministered doses greater than olanzapine 18 mg with fluoxetine 75 mg have not been evaluated [cite: 77]

Chemotherapy Associated Nausea or Vomiting (Off-label) [cite: 78]
Off-label use for prevention of chemotherapy associated nausea or vomiting in combination with 5-HT3 antagonist and dexamethasone [cite: 79]
• Breakthrough nausea and vomiting: 5-10 mg PO qDay for 3 days, when not used for acute and delayed emesis prevention [cite: 80]
• Acute and delayed emesis prevention: In combination with dexamethasone and a 5-HT3 antagonist (eg, palonosetron, aprepitant) [cite: 85, 86, 87]
  - High emetic risk IV chemotherapy: 10 mg PO the day of chemotherapy (day 1), followed by 10 mg PO qDay (days 2-4) [cite: 88]
  - Moderate emetic risk IV chemotherapy: 10 mg PO the day of chemotherapy (day 1), followed by 10 mg PO qDay (days 2-3) [cite: 89]

Dosing Modifications [cite: 92]
• Renal impairment: Dose adjustment not necessary [cite: 93, 94]
• Hepatic impairment: Dose adjustment may be necessary; use caution [cite: 95, 96]
• Slow metabolism, initial dose for schizophrenia: Initiate with 5 mg PO qDay for patients with risk factors which may slow olanzapine metabolism (eg, nonsmoking females >=65 yr). Dose escalation should performed with caution [cite: 97, 98, 99]

PEDIATRIC [cite: 100]
Dosage Forms & Strengths [cite: 101]
• tablet (Zyprexa, generic): 2.5mg, 5mg, 7.5mg, 10mg, 15mg, 20mg [cite: 102, 103, 104, 105, 106, 107, 108]
• tablet, orally disintegrating (Zyprexa Zydis): 5mg, 10mg, 15mg, 20mg [cite: 109, 110, 111, 112, 117]

Schizophrenia [cite: 118]
Indicated for treatment of schizophrenia, as part of a total treatment program that includes psychological, educational, and social interventions [cite: 119, 120]
• <13 years: Safety and efficacy not established [cite: 121]
• 13-17 years: Dose selection: 2.5-5 mg daily, with a target dose of 10 mg/day within several days [cite: 122, 123, 124]
  - If further dosage adjustments are indicated, wait at least 1 week; steady state not typically achieved until ~1 week [cite: 125]
  - Adjust dose increments/decrements by 2.5-5 mg daily. Doses >20 mg/day have not been evaluated [cite: 126, 127]
• Maintenance treatment: Extrapolated from adult data. Responding patients may continue beyond acute treatment at lowest dose needed to maintain remission. Periodically reevaluate [cite: 128, 129, 130, 131, 132]

Bipolar I Disorder (Manic or Mixed Episodes) [cite: 133]
Indicated for acute treatment of manic or mixed episodes associated with bipolar I disorder and maintenance treatment [cite: 134]
• <13 years: Safety and efficacy not established [cite: 135]
• 13-17 years: 2.5-5 mg daily, target dose 10 mg/day. Adjust dose increments/decrements by 2.5-5 mg daily [cite: 136, 137, 138, 141]
• Maintenance treatment: Extrapolated from adult data [cite: 147, 148, 149]

Bipolar I Disorder (Depressive Episodes) [cite: 152]
• <10 years: Safety and efficacy not established [cite: 154]
• 10-17 years: Olanzapine 2.5 mg PO qPM and fluoxetine 20 mg PO qPM initially [cite: 155]

Stuttering (Off-label) [cite: 158]
• <=12 years: 1.25 mg PO at bedtime for 4 weeks, then 2.5 mg at bedtime [cite: 159]
• >12 years: 2.5 mg PO at bedtime for 4 weeks, then 5 mg at bedtime [cite: 160]

Dosing Considerations [cite: 161]
Consider potential long-term risks when prescribing to adolescents (e.g., weight gain, dyslipidemia) and possible use of alternative drugs [cite: 162]

GERIATRIC [cite: 163]
Not approved for dementia-related psychosis, because of increased risk of cardiovascular or infection-related mortality. Consider lower starting dosage [cite: 164, 165]
• Schizophrenia: 2.5-5 mg/day PO initially [cite: 166, 167]
• IM (extended-release): 150 mg every 4 weeks in patients who are debilitated or predisposed to hypotensive episodes [cite: 168]
• Schizophrenia or Bipolar-Related Agitation: IM (short-acting): 5 mg; consider 2.5 mg if patient is predisposed to hypotensive reactions [cite: 170, 171, 175]

Monitor Closely (417) [cite: 533]
• Anticholinergic combinations, belladonna alkaloids, dicyclomine, hyoscyamine, oxybutynin, scopolamine, etc.: Additive anticholinergic effects [cite: 582-1616]
• CNS Depressants (alprazolam, baclofen, buprenorphine, carisoprodol, chlordiazepoxide, clonazepam, codeine, diazepam, diphenhydramine, ethanol, fentanyl, gabapentin, lorazepam, methadone, morphine, oxycodone, propofol, tramadol, zolpidem, etc.): Additive sedation and potential CNS depression [cite: 561-1844]

• Antidiabetic agents (acarbose, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, insulins, metformin, pioglitazone, sitagliptin): Atypical antipsychotics have been associated with hyperglycemia that may alter blood glucose control; monitor glucose levels closely [cite: 537-1634]
• Antihypertensives (benazepril, captopril, clonidine): Enhanced hypotensive effects [cite: 650-762]

WARNINGS [cite: 1921]
Black Box Warnings [cite: 1926]
• Not approved for dementia-related psychosis; elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis who are treated with antipsychotic drugs are at increased risk of death (cardiovascular or infectious) [cite: 1927, 1928]
• Patients receiving olanzapine extended-release IM injection are at risk for severe sedation (including coma) or delirium after each injection and must be observed for at least 3 hours in registered facility [cite: 1929, 1930]

Contraindications [cite: 1931]
• Documented hypersensitivity [cite: 1932]

Cautions [cite: 1934]
• Possibility of suicide attempt is inherent in schizophrenia and bipolar I disorder [cite: 1935]
• Irreversible, involuntary, dyskinetic movements (Tardive dyskinesia) may develop [cite: 1937, 1939]
• Neutropenia, leukopenia, and agranulocytosis reported [cite: 1942]
• Cerebrovascular effects including stroke and TIA resulting in death reported in elderly with dementia [cite: 1943, 1944]
• Drug Reaction with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS) reported; sometimes fatal [cite: 1946, 1948]
• May cause somnolence, postural hypotension, motor and sensory instability, leading to falls [cite: 1952]
• REMS program applies to extended release IM formulation [cite: 1955-1959]
• Seizures: Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures [cite: 1960, 1962]
• Metabolic changes: Weight gain, Hyperglycemia, Diabetes mellitus, Hyperlipidemia [cite: 1969-1980]
• Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS): hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status [cite: 1981-1983]

PREGNANCY & LACTATION [cite: 1987]
• Pregnancy category: C [cite: 1988, 1995]
• Neonates exposed to antipsychotic drugs during 3rd trimester of pregnancy are at risk for EPS or withdrawal symptoms after delivery [cite: 1989, 1990]
• Lactation: Drug enters breast milk; not recommended [cite: 1991]

PHARMACOLOGY [cite: 2003]
• Mechanism of Action: May act through combination of dopamine and serotonin type 2 receptor site antagonism [cite: 2004, 2005]
• Absorption: Peak plasma time: 6 hr (PO); 15-45 min (short-acting IM); 7 days (extended-release IM) [cite: 2006, 2007]
• Distribution: Protein bound: 93%, Vd: 1000 L [cite: 2008-2010]
• Metabolism: Extensively metabolized through direct glucuronidation and CYP450 oxidation (Metabolites: Inactive) [cite: 2011-2013]
• Elimination: Half-life: 21-54 hr (immediate release); 30 days (extended release). Excretion: Urine (57%), feces (30%) [cite: 2014-2016]

ADMINISTRATION & STORAGE [cite: 2017, 2036]
• Oral administration: May take with or without food. Oral disintegrating tablets dissolve rapidly in saliva [cite: 2018-2023]
• IM Administration: Short-acting and extended-release IM preparations are not interchangeable [cite: 2024, 2025]
• Storage: Store at room temperature, 68-77°F (20-25°C). Protect from light and moisture [cite: 2038, 2039]
