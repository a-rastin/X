quetiapine (Rx) [cite: 3]
Brand and Other Names: Seroquel, Seroquel XR [cite: 4]
Classes: Antipsychotics, 2nd Generation; Antimanic Agents [cite: 4]

Dosing & Uses [cite: 5]
ADULT [cite: 6]
Dosage Forms & Strengths [cite: 7]
• tablet, immediate release: 25mg, 50mg, 100mg, 200mg, 300mg, 400mg [cite: 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14]
• tablet, extended release: 50mg, 150mg, 200mg, 300mg, 400mg [cite: 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20]

Schizophrenia [cite: 21]
• Immediate release [cite: 22]
  - Day 1: 50 mg/day PO divided q12hr [cite: 23]
  - Days 2-3: Dose increased daily in increments of 25-50 mg q8-12hr to 300-400 mg by day 4; further adjustments can be made in increments of 25-50 mg q12hr at intervals >=2 days [cite: 24, 25]
  - Dosage range: 150-750 mg/day [cite: 26]
• Extended release [cite: 27]
  - Day 1: 300 mg/day PO; subsequently, may be increased by up to 300 mg/day at intervals >=1 day [cite: 28]
  - retitrated following initiation of therapy; patients may reinitiate at their previous maintenance dose if discontinued therapy <1 week [cite: 33]

Bipolar I Disorder, Mania [cite: 34]
Administered as monotherapy or as adjunct to lithium or divalproex [cite: 35]
• Immediate release [cite: 36]
  - Day 1: 100 mg/day PO divided q12hr [cite: 37]
  - Day 2: 200 mg/day PO divided q12hr [cite: 38]
  - Day 3: 300 mg/day PO divided q12hr [cite: 39]
  - Day 4: 400 mg/day PO divided q12hr [cite: 40]
  - Further dosage adjustments, up to 800 mg/day by day 6, should be in increments <=200 mg/day [cite: 41]
  - Dosage range: 400-800 mg/day not to exceed 800 mg/day [cite: 42]
• Extended release [cite: 43]
  - Day 1: 300 mg PO once daily [cite: 44]
  - Day 2: 600 mg PO once daily [cite: 45]
  - Maintenance (day 3 onward): 400-800 mg/day PO [cite: 46]

Bipolar Disorder, Depressive Episodes [cite: 47]
Either immediate-release or extended-release tablets may be given; dosage titrated upward over 4 days [cite: 48]
• Day 1: 50 mg PO at bedtime [cite: 49]
• Day 2: 100 mg PO at bedtime [cite: 50]
• Day 3: 200 mg PO at bedtime [cite: 51]
• Maintenance (day 4 onward): 300 mg PO at bedtime [cite: 52]

Bipolar I Disorder, Maintenance [cite: 53]
Administered as adjunct to lithium or divalproex [cite: 54]
• Extended release: 400-800 mg/day PO in single dose [cite: 59]
• Generally, in maintenance phase, patients continue to receive same dosage on which they were stabilized [cite: 60]

Major Depressive Disorder [cite: 61]
Extended-release formulation administered as adjunct to antidepressants [cite: 62]
• Days 1 and 2: 50 mg PO in evening [cite: 63]
• Day 3: May be increased to 150 mg PO in evening [cite: 64]
• Dosage range: 150-300 mg/day [cite: 65]

Alcohol Dependence (Off-label) [cite: 66]
• 25-50 mg PO at bedtime; may be titrated; not to exceed 300 mg [cite: 67]

Insomnia (Off-label) [cite: 68]
• Usually, 25 mg/day PO at bedtime initially [cite: 69]

Administration [cite: 70]
• Preferably, take in evening without food or with light meal [cite: 71]
• Switching from immediate release to extended release: Convert to extended release tablets at equivalent immediate release total daily dose; administer once daily; individual adjustments may be necessary [cite: 72, 73, 74]

Interactions [cite: 137]
Contraindicated (4) [cite: 145]
• amisulpride: Increases risk of neuroleptic malignant syndrome. [cite: 146, 151]
• goserelin: increases toxicity of quetiapine by QTc interval. Increases risk of torsades de pointes. [cite: 152, 153]
• lefamulin: increases level/effect of quetiapine by affecting CYP3A4 metabolism. Lefamulin contraindicated with CYP3A substrates known to prolong QT interval. [cite: 154, 155, 156]
• leuprolide: increases toxicity by QTc interval. Increases risk of torsades de pointes. [cite: 157, 158]

Drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), falls, nocturnal enuresis, retrograde amnesia, SIADH, Stevens-Johnson syndrome (SJS), toxic epidermal necrolysis (TEN), decreased platelet count, serious liver reactions (hepatitis, liver necrosis, hepatic failure), agranulocytosis, intestinal obstruction, ileus, colon ischemia, urinary retention, sleep apnea, acute generalized exanthematous pustulosis (AGEP), confusional state, fecal incontinence, and cutaneous vasculitis [cite: 2180]

Warnings [cite: 2181]
Black Box Warnings [cite: 2182]
• Not approved for dementia-related psychosis; elderly patients treated with antipsychotic drugs are at increased risk of death (cardiovascular or infectious). [cite: 2183, 2184, 2200, 2201, 2216]
• Increased risk of suicidal thinking and behavior in children, adolescents, and young adults taking antidepressants; monitor and report suicidality. [cite: 2185, 2186]
• Not approved for children <10 years. [cite: 2187]

Contraindications [cite: 2188]
• Documented hypersensitivity [cite: 2189]

Cautions [cite: 2190]
• Use with caution in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease; may worsen hypotensive conditions. [cite: 2191, 2192]
• Use with caution in breast cancer and history of seizure. [cite: 2193]
• Increased risk of hyperglycemia and diabetes (ketoacidosis, hyperosmolar coma, or death). Monitor blood glucose. [cite: 2194, 2195]
• Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) and Tardive dyskinesia possible. [cite: 2202, 2203]
• Clinical worsening of depression and suicide ideation may occur. [cite: 2204]
• Hyperlipidemia and weight gain may occur; monitor fasting lipids and weight. [cite: 2205, 2206]
• Cataract may occur; monitor. [cite: 2207]
• Increased blood pressure in children and adolescents reported; monitor. [cite: 2208]
• Leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis may occur; perform CBC and discontinue if WBC declines. [cite: 2209, 2210]
• Monitor orthostatic vital signs; may cause somnolence, postural hypotension, motor and sensory instability (fall risk). [cite: 2211, 2212, 2213]
• Potential withdrawal symptoms upon abrupt discontinuance. [cite: 2214]
• False-positive urine drug screens for methadone or TCAs reported. [cite: 2215]
• Hyperprolactinemia: Incidence of shifts in prolactin levels reported; may suppress GnRH, inhibiting reproductive function. Galactorrhea, amenorrhea, gynecomastia, impotence, and decreased bone density may occur. Avoid if previously detected breast cancer. [cite: 2217-2230]
• QT interval prolongation: QT prolongation reported with acute overdose. Avoid with other QTc prolonging drugs. [cite: 2231-2233]

Pregnancy & Lactation [cite: 2234]
Pregnancy [cite: 2235]
• Pregnancy exposure registry monitors outcomes. [cite: 2236, 2237]
• Neonates exposed during third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms. Epidemiologic studies have not established a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, but untreated maternal mental illness carries risks. [cite: 2238-2246]
• May result in increased serum prolactin levels, which may reversibly reduce fertility in females. [cite: 2247]
• Animal studies showed embryo-fetal toxicity (delays in skeletal ossification, decreased fetal weights). [cite: 2252, 2253]
• Pregnancy Category C (historically): Use with caution if benefits outweigh risks. [cite: 2259-2265]

Lactation [cite: 2254]
• Present in human breast milk at <1% of maternal weight-adjusted dosage; no consistent adverse events reported in infants. Consider developmental/health benefits of breastfeeding along with clinical need. [cite: 2255-2258]

Pharmacology [cite: 2266]
Mechanism of Action [cite: 2267]
• Atypical antipsychotic related to clozapine; reduces positive and negative symptoms of psychotic disorders via antagonism of multiple neurotransmitter receptors (dopamine D1/D2, histamine H1, alpha1/alpha2-adrenergic, serotonin 5-HT1A/5-HT2). No affinity for benzodiazepine and cholinergic muscarinic receptors. [cite: 2268, 2269]
Absorption [cite: 2270]
• Bioavailability: 100% (oral solution) [cite: 2271]
• Peak plasma time: Immediate release, 1.5 hr; extended release, 6 hr [cite: 2272]
Distribution
• Vd: 6-14 L/kg [cite: 2278]
Metabolism [cite: 2279]
• Metabolized in liver by CYP3A4 [cite: 2280]
Elimination [cite: 2281]
• Half-life: Immediate release, 6 hr; extended release, 7 hr [cite: 2282]
• Excretion: Urine (73%), feces (20%) [cite: 2283]
