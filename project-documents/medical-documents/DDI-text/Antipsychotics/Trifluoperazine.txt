Warnings
Black Box Warnings
Patients with dementia-related psychosis who are treated with antipsychotic drugs are at an increased risk of death as shown in short-term controlled trials. The deaths appeared to be either cardiovascular (eg, heart failure, sudden death) or infectious (eg, pneumonia) in nature. This drug is not approved for the treatment of patients with dementia-related psychosis.

Contraindications
• Hypersensitivity to phenothiazines or excipients
• Comatose or greatly depressed states due to central nervous system depressants, existing blood dyscrasias, bone marrow depression, and preexisting liver damage

Cautions
• Thrombocytopenia, anemia, agranulocytosis, and pancytopenia reported. Warn patients to report sudden appearance of sore throat or other signs of infection.
• Cholestatic jaundice or liver damage reported.
• May increase mental and physical activity (e.g., angina patients).
• Hypotension has occurred; avoid large doses and parenteral administration in patients with impaired cardiovascular systems.
• Retinopathy has been reported.
• Antiemetic action may mask signs of toxicity of other drugs or conditions like intestinal obstruction, brain tumor, and Reye's syndrome.
• Chronic administration elevates prolactin levels.
• May interfere with thermoregulatory mechanisms (extreme heat caution).
• Use with caution in patients with glaucoma (anticholinergic effect).
• May diminish effect of oral anticoagulants.
• Phenothiazines can produce alpha-adrenergic blockade.
• May lower convulsive threshold; dose adjustments of anticonvulsants may be necessary. Discontinue 48 hours before myelography with metrizamide.
• May produce false-positive phenylketonuria (PKU) test results.
• Leukopenia, neutropenia, and agranulocytosis risk. Monitor CBC frequently during the first few months.
• Tardive dyskinesia risk (potentially irreversible, involuntary, dyskinetic movements).
• Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS) risk (hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status, autonomic instability).
• Encephalopathic syndrome risk when used with lithium.
• May impair mental and/or physical abilities.

Pregnancy & Lactation
Pregnancy
• Safety for use during pregnancy not established. Use only if essential.
• Neonates exposed during the third trimester are at risk for extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms following delivery (agitation, hypertonia, hypotonia, tremor, somnolence, respiratory distress, feeding disorder).
• Animal data: Reproductive studies in rats (600x human dose) showed increased malformations. Not observed at half dosage or in rabbits/monkeys.
Pregnancy Categories: C (Use with caution if benefits outweigh risks. Animal studies show risk and human studies not available or neither animal nor human studies done.)

Lactation
• Excreted in breast milk. Decision should be made to discontinue nursing or discontinue the drug, taking into account importance of drug to the mother.

Pharmacology
Mechanism of Action
Piperazine phenothiazine agent; antagonist for the postsynaptic mesolimbic dopaminergic D2 receptors in the brain; decreases the release of hypothalamic and hypophyseal hormones.

Pharmacokinetics
• Half-Life elimination: 24 hr
• Metabolism: Liver
