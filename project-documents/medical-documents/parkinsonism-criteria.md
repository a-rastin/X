# Medication-Induced Parkinsonism

## Diagnostic features

Identify **bradykinesia/akinesia, rigidity, or parkinsonian tremor** with a plausible medication relationship. Onset is commonly within weeks of initiation or dose escalation, but later onset occurs. Tremor may be absent; symmetry is not required. 

## Clinical assessment and exclusions

Document observed slowness, passive muscle tone, resting tremor, facial expression, gait, and arm swing. Review the complete medication history and symptoms before exposure.

Consider Parkinson disease, another neurological disorder, and psychiatric explanations such as catatonia, negative symptoms, or depressive slowing. Medication exposure can unmask underlying Parkinson disease. Symmetry and timing support clinical judgment but do not reliably distinguish drug-induced parkinsonism from Parkinson disease.
Improvement after a clinician-directed medication change supports causation, but recovery can be delayed and persistence warrants reassessment. Dopamine-transporter imaging may help selected diagnostically uncertain cases; it is not a routine confirmation test for every patient. 

## Assessment tool and documentation

The **Simpson–Angus Scale (SAS)** measures parkinsonian signs during treatment; a score alone does not establish etiology. Record medication changes, onset, examination findings, falls or functional limitations, alternative explanations, and follow-up findings.

Fever, altered consciousness, autonomic instability, and severe generalized rigidity require urgent evaluation for neuroleptic malignant syndrome rather than attribution to uncomplicated parkinsonism.

## Simpson–Angus Scale (SAS)

The SAS is a **10-item, clinician-rated physical examination** for antipsychotic- or other dopamine-blocking-drug-induced parkinsonian signs. It emphasizes rigidity; it is not a patient questionnaire and does not adequately assess every extrapyramidal syndrome. Use the same examination conditions and, when possible, the same trained rater for serial assessments.

### Suggested questions (not scored)

The original SAS has no standardized interview questions. Before the examination, prompts such as the following can provide clinical context but **must not be added to the SAS score**:

1. Since the medication was started or changed, have you felt slower or stiffer?
2. Have your hands, arms, legs, jaw, or body been shaking, especially while resting?
3. Have you noticed smaller steps, shuffling, reduced arm swing, difficulty starting or stopping, or falls?
4. Have you had excess saliva, drooling, or difficulty speaking because of saliva?
5. When did each symptom begin, and does it change after a medication dose?

### Physical examination and item scoring

Score every item from **0 to 4** using the item-specific anchors below. Observe spontaneous movement before eliciting signs. Examine both sides and document asymmetry, pain, arthritis, injury, or another condition that may affect the result. Do not perform a maneuver that is unsafe for the patient.

#### 1. Gait

**Ph/E:** Observe the patient walking at a natural pace. Assess posture, step pattern, and arm swing.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Normal gait and arm swing. |
| 1 | Reduced arm swing while walking. |
| 2 | Markedly reduced arm swing with evident arm rigidity. |
| 3 | Stiff gait with the arms held rigidly in front of the abdomen. |
| 4 | Stooped, shuffling gait with propulsion and retropulsion. |

#### 2. Arm dropping

**Ph/E:** The patient and examiner raise their arms to shoulder height and let them fall to their sides. Compare the speed of the patient's fall, audible contact with the trunk, and rebound with the examiner's movement.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Free fall with a clear slap against the side and rebound. |
| 1 | Slightly slowed fall, quieter contact, and little rebound. |
| 2 | Slowed fall without rebound. |
| 3 | Markedly slowed fall without an audible slap. |
| 4 | The arms descend very slowly, as if moving against strong resistance. |

#### 3. Shoulder shaking

**Ph/E:** Flex one elbow to 90 degrees. Hold the patient's hand and elbow, move the upper arm back and forth, and externally rotate the humerus; repeat on the other side. Judge resistance to passive movement.

#### 4. Elbow rigidity

**Ph/E:** With the elbow flexed to 90 degrees, passively flex and extend it while observing and palpating the biceps; repeat on the other side. Judge resistance.

#### 5. Wrist rigidity (fixation of position)

**Ph/E:** Stabilize the wrist and hold the fingers. Passively move the wrist through flexion, extension, radial deviation, and ulnar deviation; repeat on the other side. Judge resistance.

Use the same anchors for items 3–5:

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Normal tone and passive movement. |
| 1 | Slight stiffness and resistance. |
| 2 | Moderate stiffness and resistance. |
| 3 | Marked rigidity that makes passive movement difficult. |
| 4 | Extreme rigidity; the examined joint is nearly fixed. |

#### 6. Leg pendulousness

**Ph/E:** With the patient seated securely on an examination table and the lower legs hanging freely, grasp an ankle, partially extend the knee, and release the lower leg. Assess resistance to falling and the subsequent pendular swing; repeat on the other side.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | The leg swings freely. |
| 1 | Slight reduction in pendular swing. |
| 2 | Moderate resistance to swinging. |
| 3 | Marked resistance with rapid damping of the swing. |
| 4 | No pendular swing. |

#### 7. Head dropping

**Ph/E:** With the patient supine on a well-padded examination surface, support and gently raise the head, then withdraw the hand and observe the fall. Omit this maneuver if neck disease, injury, pain, instability, or another safety concern is present.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | The head falls fully and promptly onto the padded surface. |
| 1 | Slight slowing, mainly evident from softer contact with the surface. |
| 2 | Clearly visible moderate slowing. |
| 3 | The head descends stiffly and slowly. |
| 4 | The rigid neck prevents the head from reaching the surface. |

#### 8. Glabellar tap

**Ph/E:** Ask the patient to keep the eyes open and try not to blink. Tap the glabella steadily and rapidly, then count consecutive blinks.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | 0–5 blinks. |
| 1 | 6–10 blinks. |
| 2 | 11–15 blinks. |
| 3 | 16–20 blinks. |
| 4 | 21 or more blinks. |

#### 9. Tremor

**Ph/E:** Observe the patient while walking into the room and again during the examination, including while the limbs are at rest.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No tremor. |
| 1 | Mild finger tremor that is visible and palpable. |
| 2 | Intermittent tremor of a hand or arm. |
| 3 | Persistent tremor of one or more limbs. |
| 4 | Generalized or whole-body tremor. |

#### 10. Salivation

**Ph/E:** Observe the patient while speaking, then ask the patient to open the mouth and elevate the tongue. Assess pooling of saliva, interference with speech, and drooling.

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Normal salivation. |
| 1 | Excess saliva pools only when the mouth is open and the tongue is raised. |
| 2 | Excess saliva is present and occasionally may interfere with speech. |
| 3 | Speech is difficult because of excess saliva. |
| 4 | Overt, persistent drooling. |

### Scoring and interpretation

- **Item score:** 0 = normal/absent and 4 = most severe for that item.
- **Raw total:** add the 10 item scores; range **0–40**. Higher scores indicate more severe parkinsonian signs.
- **Original global (mean) score:** raw total divided by 10; range **0–4**.
- The original convention considered a mean score **up to 0.3** within the normal range; a mean **greater than 0.3** (raw total **4 or more**) was considered abnormal.
- A later validation study in a chronic, antipsychotic-treated schizophrenia population proposed **0.65** as a screening threshold (operationally, because scores increase by 0.1, a mean of **0.7 or greater**, equivalent to a raw total of **7 or more**). This is not a universal diagnostic cutoff and should not replace clinical assessment.
- The original SAS does **not** define validated mild, moderate, and severe total-score bands. Avoid assigning such labels from locally reproduced cutoffs unless the source and intended population are stated.
- Do not calculate a standard total or mean when an item is missing unless the applicable protocol provides a prespecified missing-data rule. Record why the item was not assessed.

The SAS quantifies signs at one point in time. Interpret change from baseline alongside medication timing, functional impact, differential diagnosis, and follow-up; neither a threshold nor improvement after treatment independently proves medication causation.
