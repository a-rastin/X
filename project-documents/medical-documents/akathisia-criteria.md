# Medication-Induced Acute Akathisia

## Diagnostic features

Assess these features together:

1. **Subjective restlessness plus observed motor restlessness**, such as pacing, leg movements, or inability to remain still.
2. **Medication relationship:** usually begins within **4 weeks** of initiation or dose escalation; occasionally follows reduction of medication controlling extrapyramidal symptoms.

## Clinical assessment and exclusions

Ask specifically about an internal urge to move, distress when remaining still, and relief from movement. Observe the patient both seated and standing. Establish the sequence of medication changes and symptoms; review antiemetics and antidepressants as well as antipsychotics.

Evaluate anxiety, psychotic or mood-related agitation, restless legs syndrome, intoxication or withdrawal, and neurological or medical explanations. Restlessness alone is insufficient to attribute symptoms to medication. If subjective and observed findings disagree, document uncertainty and reassess rather than diagnosing solely from pacing or anxiety.

## Assessment tool and documentation

The **Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS)** helps record objective movements, subjective awareness, distress, and global severity; it supports clinical assessment rather than independently establishing medication causation. Record the suspected medication, dose changes, onset, examination, distress, functional impact, and competing explanations.

## Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale (BARS)

The BARS is a clinician-rated, four-item instrument for assessing drug-induced akathisia. It combines observation with the patient's subjective experience. Use it to identify and grade symptoms and to follow change over time; it does not by itself establish medication causation or replace the diagnostic assessment above.

### Administration and physical examination

1. Observe the patient **seated for at least 2 minutes** while engaged in neutral conversation.
2. Observe the patient **standing for at least 2 minutes** while engaged in neutral conversation.
3. Note relevant movements seen at other times, such as walking into the room or participating in routine activity.
4. After observation, ask directly about inner restlessness, the urge to move, and associated distress.

During the examination, look for leg or foot shuffling, stamping, swinging a leg while seated, rocking from foot to foot, walking in place, repeated standing or pacing, and inability to remain seated or standing. Separate these characteristic movements from occasional nonspecific fidgeting.

### Suggested interview questions

The original BARS specifies direct questioning but does not prescribe a fixed question script. The following prompts cover its subjective domains:

1. Do you feel restless inside or feel that you need to move?
2. Is the feeling mainly in your legs, or elsewhere in your body?
3. Do you find it difficult to keep your legs still?
4. Do you have a strong urge to walk or pace?
5. Is the restlessness worse when you must stand or sit still?
6. How much of the time do you experience the urge to move?
7. How distressing is the restlessness: not at all, mildly, moderately, or severely?
8. Does it interfere with sitting, lying down, sleeping, work, or other activities?

These prompts assist administration and are not additional scored items.

### Item 1: Objective restlessness (0–3)

| Score | Rating anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Normal behavior or only occasional fidgeting of the limbs. |
| 1 | Characteristic restless movements are present for **less than half** of the observation period. Examples include leg or foot shuffling, stamping, swinging one leg while seated, rocking from foot to foot, or walking in place while standing. |
| 2 | The characteristic movements described above are present for **at least half** of the observation period. |
| 3 | Characteristic movements are virtually constant, or the patient cannot remain seated or standing without walking or pacing. |

### Item 2: Subjective awareness of restlessness (0–3)

| Score | Rating anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No inner restlessness. |
| 1 | Nonspecific inner restlessness. |
| 2 | Awareness of difficulty keeping the legs still, a desire to move the legs, or inner restlessness that becomes worse when required to stand still. |
| 3 | An intense compulsion to move most of the time or a strong desire to walk or pace most of the time. |

### Item 3: Distress related to restlessness (0–3)

| Score | Rating anchor |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No distress. |
| 1 | Mild distress. |
| 2 | Moderate distress. |
| 3 | Severe distress. |

### Item 4: Global clinical assessment (0–5)

Rate the overall syndrome using the observation, subjective report, and clinical judgment together.

| Score | Interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0 | **Absent.** No subjective awareness of restlessness. Characteristic movements without subjective inner restlessness or a compulsive urge to move are classified in the original scale as **pseudoakathisia**. |
| 1 | **Questionable.** Nonspecific inner tension with fidgeting. |
| 2 | **Mild.** Leg or inner restlessness, especially when required to stand still; characteristic movements need not be present beyond fidgeting, and distress is minimal or absent. |
| 3 | **Moderate.** Subjective restlessness plus characteristic movements, such as rocking from foot to foot; the condition is distressing. |
| 4 | **Marked.** A compelling desire to walk or pace with obvious distress, although the patient can remain seated for at least 5 minutes. |
| 5 | **Severe.** A strong urge to pace most of the time, inability to sit or lie for more than a few minutes, constant restlessness, intense distress, and insomnia. |

### Scoring and interpretation

- Record all four item scores separately: **objective /3, awareness /3, distress /3, global /5**.
- The first three item scores may be summed as a descriptive **component total from 0 to 9**. This total is useful for tracking change, but the original author regarded the global item as the principal overall severity measure.
- A **global score of 2 or greater** indicates that akathisia is present according to the BARS threshold: 2 mild, 3 moderate, 4 marked, and 5 severe.
- A global score of 2 can be assigned from a characteristic subjective report even when the only visible movement is nonspecific fidgeting.
- Objective movements without subjective restlessness are recorded as pseudoakathisia in the original BARS. Reassess the history, differential diagnosis, and longitudinal course rather than assuming acute medication-induced akathisia.
- The BARS severity threshold does not establish that a medication caused the syndrome. Apply the medication timing and exclusion criteria above separately.

Some reproduced BARS forms state that the global scale ranges from 0 to 4, but the published anchors include **0 through 5**. The correct global range is therefore **0–5**.
