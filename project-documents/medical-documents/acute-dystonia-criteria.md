# Medication-Induced Acute Dystonia

## Diagnostic features

Identify **sustained involuntary contractions and abnormal postures** associated with a causative medication. Onset is usually within days of initiation, rapid dose escalation, or reduction of medication preventing extrapyramidal symptoms.

Typical findings include neck twisting, jaw spasm, tongue protrusion, sustained eye deviation (oculogyric crisis), or abnormal limb or trunk postures. Involvement of throat muscles can impair speech, swallowing, or breathing. 

## Clinical assessment and exclusions

Establish the medication timeline, including antipsychotics, dopamine-blocking antiemetics, and changes to preventive anticholinergic medication. Assess the distribution and persistence of the abnormal postures.

Exclude catatonia, a primary neurological or medical cause, and a tardive movement disorder. Symptoms preceding medication exposure, unexplained focal neurological findings, or progression unrelated to medication changes warrant further investigation. A rapid response to appropriate acute treatment supports the impression, but does not replace differential diagnosis. 

## Urgency and documentation

**Stridor or breathing difficulty can indicate laryngeal dystonia and requires emergency assessment.** Do not delay airway evaluation to complete a diagnostic checklist.
