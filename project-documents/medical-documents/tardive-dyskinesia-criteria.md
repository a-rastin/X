# Tardive Dyskinesia

## Diagnostic features

Assess these features together:

1. **Movement pattern:** involuntary choreiform, athetoid, or stereotyped movements involving the mouth, tongue, trunk, or limbs.
2. **Persistence:** movements occur over **at least 4 weeks**.
3. **Exposure:** a dopamine receptor blocker was used for **at least 3 months**, or **1 month at age 60 or older**.
4. **Onset:** during exposure, or within **4 weeks after oral withdrawal** or **8 weeks after long-acting injectable withdrawal**.

## Differential diagnosis

Exclude other explanations, including spontaneous dyskinesia, Huntington disease, Wilson disease, dental problems, and other medication-induced movements. Distinguish irregular dyskinesia from parkinsonian tremor and from acute dystonia or akathisia. Brief withdrawal-associated movements require longitudinal reassessment.

## Research criteria and assessment

**Schooler–Kane probable tardive dyskinesia** requires at least 3 months of cumulative antipsychotic exposure, no alternative cause, and either moderate movements in one body region or mild movements in two or more. On the Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS), these correspond to a regional score of **3 or greater in one region**, or **2 or greater in at least two regions**. These are research thresholds, not additional DSM requirements.

Record medication history, onset, affected regions, AIMS findings, functional impact, and alternative explanations. AIMS supports measurement; its total score alone does not establish diagnosis or determine intervention.
