# Schizophrenia: DSM-5-TR–Based Diagnostic Guideline

## Diagnostic overview

Schizophrenia is diagnosed clinically. No laboratory test, imaging study, genetic test, or rating scale independently establishes the diagnosis. The clinician must identify a characteristic psychotic syndrome, clinically important functional decline, a qualifying longitudinal course, and the absence of a better psychiatric, substance-related, medical, developmental, or cultural explanation.


All six requirements below must be satisfied.

## Required criteria

### A. Characteristic symptoms

During the same active-phase interval, the person has **at least two** of the following five symptom domains for a substantial part of **1 month**. The active interval may be shorter when effective intervention abbreviates it.

1. **Delusions:** fixed beliefs that remain despite compelling contrary evidence and are not ordinarily accepted within the person's cultural or religious context.
2. **Hallucinations:** perception-like experiences occurring without a corresponding external stimulus, experienced with the vividness and impact of ordinary perception.
3. **Disorganized speech:** speech that reflects materially disordered thought, such as frequent loss of associations, marked tangentiality, or incoherence.
4. **Markedly disorganized or catatonic behavior:** behavior severe enough to interfere with goal-directed activity or self-care, or a qualifying catatonic syndrome.
5. **Negative symptoms:** especially reduced emotional expression or markedly reduced initiation and persistence of purposeful activity. Alogia, anhedonia, and asociality may also be clinically relevant.

At least one qualifying symptom must be **delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech**. Negative symptoms and disorganized/catatonic behavior alone are insufficient.

### B. Functional decline

For a meaningful portion of the illness, functioning in at least one major area—such as work, education, relationships, or self-care—is clearly below the person's prior level.

When onset occurs before adult roles are established, document failure to reach the expected interpersonal, academic, or occupational level rather than decline from an adult baseline.

### C. Duration

Continuous evidence of illness persists for **at least 6 months**. This period must contain:

- at least **1 month of active-phase symptoms** meeting Criterion A, unless effective intervention shortened that phase; and
- any remaining time made up of prodromal, residual, or additional active symptoms.

During prodromal or residual periods, illness may be represented by negative symptoms alone or by at least two attenuated Criterion A symptoms, such as unusual perceptual experiences or odd beliefs that do not reach full psychotic intensity.

The six-month clock concerns continuous signs of the disturbance, not six uninterrupted months of full-intensity psychosis.

### D. Mood-disorder and schizoaffective exclusion

Rule out schizoaffective disorder and depressive or bipolar disorders with psychotic features. Schizophrenia remains possible when:

- no major depressive or manic episode occurred at the same time as the active psychotic syndrome; or
- mood episodes did occur during the illness, but together occupied only a **minority** of the total active and residual course.

This judgment requires a longitudinal timeline. A cross-sectional interview during psychosis is usually insufficient.

### E. Substance, medication, and medical exclusion

The syndrome must not be better explained by the physiological effects of a substance, a prescribed or over-the-counter medication, toxin exposure, withdrawal state, or another medical or neurological condition.

Assess the timing of symptoms against exposure, intoxication, withdrawal, medication changes, delirium, neurological findings, and the course after the suspected cause resolves. Testing should be selected from the history, examination, age, onset pattern, and clinical setting; there is no universal test panel that confirms schizophrenia.

### F. Autism-spectrum and childhood communication disorders

If autism spectrum disorder or a childhood-onset communication disorder is already present, diagnose schizophrenia additionally only when **prominent delusions or hallucinations** are present together with the other required schizophrenia features for at least **1 month**, or less if effective intervention shortened the active phase.
