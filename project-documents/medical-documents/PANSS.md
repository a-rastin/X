# Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS)

PANSS is a **clinician-rated** measure of schizophrenia-spectrum symptom severity.

Rate symptoms present during the **previous 7 days**, using the interview and available collateral information.

## Questions

### Positive symptoms (P)

| Item | Question prompt |
|---|---|
| P1. Delusions | Does the person hold fixed, implausible beliefs that are not shared by the person’s cultural or social context and are resistant to evidence? |
| P2. Conceptual disorganization | Is the person’s thinking or speech difficult to follow because of loose associations, tangentiality, incoherence, or derailment? |
| P3. Hallucinatory behavior | Does the person report or appear to respond to perceptual experiences without an external stimulus? |
| P4. Excitement | Is there unusually increased activation, energy, motor activity, or emotional intensity? |
| P5. Grandiosity | Does the person express an exaggerated sense of power, importance, ability, identity, or status? |
| P6. Suspiciousness/persecution | Does the person show suspiciousness or beliefs that others intend to harm, exploit, monitor, or persecute them? |
| P7. Hostility | Does the person show verbal or physical hostility, anger, resentment, or aggression toward others? |

### Negative symptoms (N)

| Item | Question prompt |
|---|---|
| N1. Blunted affect | Is the person’s emotional expression noticeably reduced in facial expression, voice, gestures, or emotional responsiveness? |
| N2. Emotional withdrawal | Does the person show reduced involvement in relationships and limited emotional contact with others? |
| N3. Poor rapport | Is it difficult to establish a natural, cooperative, and empathetic interpersonal relationship with the person? |
| N4. Passive/apathetic social withdrawal | Does the person show reduced interest, initiative, or participation in social interaction? |
| N5. Difficulty in abstract thinking | Does the person have difficulty moving beyond concrete or literal thinking when asked to interpret concepts, similarities, or proverbs? |
| N6. Lack of spontaneity and flow of conversation | Is the person’s conversation reduced in spontaneous initiation, productivity, or conversational flow? |
| N7. Stereotyped thinking | Is the person’s thinking repetitive, rigid, simplistic, or stereotyped? |

### General psychopathology (G)

| Item | Question prompt |
|---|---|
| G1. Somatic concern | Is the person excessively concerned about physical health, bodily symptoms, or illness? |
| G2. Anxiety | Does the person show excessive fear, worry, apprehension, or nervousness? |
| G3. Guilt feelings | Does the person experience excessive or inappropriate guilt, self-blame, or remorse? |
| G4. Tension | Does the person show observable or reported physical and psychological tension, restlessness, or agitation? |
| G5. Mannerisms and posturing | Are there unusual, odd, artificial, or socially inappropriate movements, gestures, poses, or mannerisms? |
| G6. Depression | Does the person show a depressed mood, hopelessness, sadness, or reduced interest and pleasure? |
| G7. Motor retardation | Is there a noticeable slowing of movement, speech, or activity? |
| G8. Uncooperativeness | Does the person resist the interview, treatment, or reasonable requests without adequate explanation? |
| G9. Unusual thought content | Does the person express unusual, implausible, or bizarre ideas that do not meet the threshold for a fixed delusion? |
| G10. Disorientation | Is the person confused about time, place, person, or the current situation? |
| G11. Poor attention | Does the person have difficulty sustaining, shifting, or directing attention during the interview? |
| G12. Lack of judgment and insight | Does the person have impaired understanding of their condition, symptoms, consequences, or need for help? |
| G13. Disturbance of volition | Is there reduced ability to initiate, sustain, or direct purposeful activity? |
| G14. Poor impulse control | Does the person have difficulty controlling urges or behavior, with risk of sudden or poorly considered actions? |
| G15. Preoccupation | Is the person excessively absorbed in internal thoughts, feelings, or ideas in a way that interferes with the interview or functioning? |
| G16. Active social avoidance | Does the person deliberately avoid social contact because of fear, distrust, hostility, or unusual beliefs? |

## Scoring system

Score **each item from 1 to 7**. A score of 1 means the symptom is absent; higher scores indicate greater severity.

| Score | Rating | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Absent | No evidence of the symptom during the rating period. |
| 2 | Minimal | Questionable, subtle, or very limited evidence. |
| 3 | Mild | Clearly present but causes little interference. |
| 4 | Moderate | Clearly present and causes meaningful interference. |
| 5 | Moderate–severe | Prominent symptom with substantial interference. |
| 6 | Severe | Marked symptom that seriously disrupts functioning or interaction. |
| 7 | Extreme | Very severe, pervasive, or disabling symptom; may require close supervision. |

Calculate sums without reverse-scoring:

| Scale | Items | Score range |
|---|---|---:|
| Positive | P1–P7 | 7–49 |
| Negative | N1–N7 | 7–49 |
| General psychopathology | G1–G16 | 16–112 |
| **Total PANSS** | **All 30 items** | **30–210** |

Higher scores indicate greater symptom burden. Record item scores and subscale scores, not only the total score.

## Interpretation

### Approximate total-score anchors

These are approximate associations with clinician-rated global severity, not diagnostic thresholds or treatment rules:

| Total PANSS | Approximate interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 30–57 | Below the commonly cited mild-illness anchor |
| 58–74 | Mildly ill |
| 75–94 | Moderately ill |
| 95–115 | Markedly ill |
| ≥116 | Severely ill |

Interpret the total together with the subscales, individual high-scoring items, functional impairment, risk, duration, and collateral information. A PANSS score alone cannot establish schizophrenia, distinguish all causes of psychosis, or determine an individual treatment plan.

For follow-up, compare the same domains over time. Percentage change can be calculated as:

```text
(baseline total − follow-up total) ÷ baseline total × 100
```
