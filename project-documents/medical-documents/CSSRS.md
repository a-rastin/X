# Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS)

The C-SSRS is a clinician-administered assessment of suicidal ideation and suicidal behavior.

## Questions

### 1. Suicidal ideation severity

Ask each item in order. A positive response to a more severe item implies endorsement of the lower levels, but each response and its time window should still be documented. The wording below is a concise paraphrase; use the authorized form for exact administration wording and probes.

| Level | Construct | Paraphrased question |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Wish to be dead | Have you wished you were dead, or wished you could go to sleep and not wake up? |
| 2 | Nonspecific active thoughts | Have you had thoughts of killing yourself, without thinking about a method, intent, or plan? |
| 3 | Method, without intent | Have you thought about how you might kill yourself, without intending to act on those thoughts? |
| 4 | Some intent, no specific plan | Have you had suicidal thoughts and some intention of acting on them, but without a specific plan? |
| 5 | Specific plan and intent | Have you worked out a method or plan for killing yourself, and do you intend to carry it out? |

For each positive response, document when it occurred, whether it is current or historical, the person’s own description, and the clinician’s determination of whether the criterion is met.

### 2. Intensity of ideation

If any ideation is endorsed, rate the most severe type of ideation identified above and assess the following dimensions. These are **not added to the 0–5 severity level**.

| Dimension | Question focus | Typical coding |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | How often do the thoughts occur? | 1 = less than weekly; 2 = once weekly; 3 = 2–5 days/week; 4 = daily/almost daily; 5 = many times daily |
| Duration | When present, how long do they last? | 1 = seconds/minutes; 2 = less than an hour; 3 = 1–4 hours; 4 = 4–8 hours/most of the day; 5 = more than 8 hours or persistent |
| Controllability | Can the person stop or control the thoughts? | 1 = easily; 2 = with little difficulty; 3 = with some difficulty; 4 = with much difficulty; 5 = unable; 0 = does not try |
| Deterrents | What has stopped the person from acting? | 1 = definitely stopped; 2 = probably stopped; 3 = uncertain; 4 = probably did not stop; 5 = definitely did not stop; 0 = not applicable |
| Reasons | Why does the person have the thoughts? | 1 = entirely attention/reaction; 2 = mostly attention/reaction; 3 = both equally; 4 = mostly to end pain; 5 = entirely to end pain; 0 = not applicable |

### 3. Suicidal behavior

Ask about each category separately and record whether it occurred, when, how many times, the person’s intent, and the most recent event. Do not treat a behavior category as absent merely because there was no injury.

| Category | Question focus |
|---|---|
| Actual suicide attempt | Did the person engage in potentially self-injurious behavior with at least some intent to die? Injury is not required. |
| Interrupted attempt | Did another person or an outside circumstance stop the person after the attempt had begun but before the self-injurious act occurred? |
| Aborted attempt | Did the person begin steps toward an attempt but stop themselves before the self-injurious act occurred? |
| Preparatory acts or behavior | Did the person take steps toward an attempt, such as obtaining or arranging a method, writing a suicide note, or giving away possessions? |
| Nonsuicidal self-injury | Did the person intentionally injure themselves without suicidal intent? Document separately from suicidal behavior. |

Also document whether suicidal behavior occurred during the defined recent period. For an actual attempt, record actual medical lethality/damage using the authorized form’s 0–5 coding and, when actual damage is 0, potential lethality using its 0–2 coding. Lethality is not a substitute for assessing intent or current risk.

## Scoring system

### Ideation severity

The ideation severity result is the **highest-numbered ideation level endorsed** during the defined assessment period:

| Result | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No level 1–5 ideation endorsed |
| 1 | Wish to be dead |
| 2 | Nonspecific active suicidal thoughts |
| 3 | Suicidal thoughts involving a method, without intent to act |
| 4 | Suicidal thoughts with some intent to act, without a specific plan |
| 5 | Suicidal thoughts with a specific plan and intent |

This 0–5 result is an ordinal severity indicator, not a probability of suicide and not a standalone disposition rule. It should be stored with the time window and current-versus-lifetime designation. A response of “yes” to level 4 or 5 is especially concerning when recent or current.

### Intensity and behavior

- Record the five intensity dimensions individually. Do not sum them into an overall risk score.
- Record behavior categories as present/absent, with dates and counts where applicable.
- Record actual lethality, potential lethality, intent, and medical consequences for attempts using the authorized form’s coding.
- Do not combine ideation severity, intensity, behavior, or lethality into an unvalidated composite score.

## Interpretation

Interpret results with the clinical interview, recent change in risk, access to means, psychiatric and medical state, substance use, history of attempts, protective factors, collateral information, and the person’s ability to participate in a safety plan. The C-SSRS identifies suicidal ideation and behavior; it does not diagnose a disorder or determine risk by itself.

For this demonstration application, use the following triage flags, subject to local policy and clinician review:

| Flag | Trigger and required interpretation |
|---|---|
| No positive C-SSRS item | No ideation or behavior was endorsed in the assessed window. Continue routine clinical assessment; a negative screen does not prove absence of risk. |
| Positive ideation / clinical review | Any level 1–3 ideation, or any historical suicidal behavior, requires documentation and clinical review. Recentness, worsening, intent, means, and protective factors determine next steps. |
| High-risk alert | A recent (typically past-month) positive level 4 or 5, and/or suicidal behavior in the recent (typically past-3-month) window, requires immediate clinician assessment and safety action under local protocol. |
| Immediate emergency concern | Current intent or plan, an attempt in progress, inability to stay safe, severe agitation/intoxication/psychosis, or imminent access to lethal means requires emergency intervention according to local policy. Do not wait for a calculated result. |

“Low,” “moderate,” and “high” are setting-specific triage categories, not universal C-SSRS score bands. The Columbia Protocol states that the most concerning findings across settings are a recent positive response to ideation level 4 or 5 and/or recent suicidal behavior; the appropriate intervention depends on the setting and available resources.
